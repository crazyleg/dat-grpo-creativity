The red light on the desk blazed with a familiar, steady pulse. For Elias, it was the only heartbeat in the studio that mattered. He adjusted his headphones, the foam cups sealing out the hum of the city outside, and clicked the microphone live.

"Good evening, folks. It’s ten p.m., and you’re listening to *The Night Shift*. We’ve got a caller in line tonight. Let’s bring them in."

The static cleared, replaced by a voice that was soft, slightly raspy, and impossibly familiar. "Hi, Elias. It’s Arthur."

Elias froze. The pen in his hand hovered over the notepad, ink pooling on the paper. He knew that voice. He had heard it every single night for three thousand, six hundred and fifty-two consecutive nights. Ten years. Not a single missed call. Not a single holiday. Not even during the hurricane that knocked out power to half the borough, or the night Elias had lost his voice to a severe laryngitis. Arthur had always been there, waiting on the other end of the line, ready to talk about his garden, his late wife, or the price of eggs.

"Arthur," Elias said, his voice barely above a whisper. "How are you?"

"Same as always, son. A bit tired, but steady. I was just thinking about the roses again. They’re blooming early this year."

It was a mundane answer. It was the same answer he had given last night, and the night before that, and the night before that. For a decade, Arthur had been the anchor of Elias’s broadcast. He was the constant in a world of fleeting trends and viral moments. Elias had built his career on the unpredictability of late-night radio, but his comfort lay in the predictability of Arthur.

But tonight, something felt different. The silence between Arthur’s words was heavier. Elias looked at the clock. 10:01 PM. The caller ID on the screen didn’t show a name, just a number. He had never checked the number before. He had never needed to. He assumed Arthur was sitting in his armchair in suburban Ohio, a cup of tea cooling in his hand.

"Arthur," Elias said, breaking the professional persona. "Are you... are you okay? You sound... distant."

A long pause stretched through the line. When Arthur spoke again, the rasp was gone. The voice was clear, young, and utterly unfamiliar. "I’m fine, Elias. I’m just... done."

The line went dead. The red light on the desk flickered and died. The studio was silent, save for the hum of the ventilation system. Elias sat there, the pen still hovering, the ink stain spreading like a dark flower on the white paper. He reached for his phone, his hands trembling. He had never asked for Arthur’s address. He had never asked for his last name. He had only ever known the voice, and now, the voice was gone, leaving behind only the echo of ten years of routine, and the terrifying realization that he had never truly known the man who had kept him company in the dark.
