The red light on the desk blinks with the same aggressive rhythm it has for the past three thousand, six hundred and fifty-two nights. Arthur adjusts his headset, the foam earpiece pressing a familiar, slightly damp circle against his ear. He checks the timestamp. 11:42 PM. It is always 11:42 PM.

"Hello, *The Midnight Frequency*," Arthur says, his voice a practiced, gravelly baritone that has become a fixture of the city’s insomnia. "We’re taking calls. If you’re listening, you’re not alone."

The line connects with a burst of static, followed by a silence so profound it feels like a physical weight in the studio. Then, the voice. It is thin, reedy, and entirely unchanged. "Hello, Arthur."

Arthur’s hand hovers over the 'hang up' button. He has done this ten thousand times. He has laughed at drunkards, cried with widows, and argued with conspiracy theorists. But he has never once considered the possibility that the voice on the other end might be the same voice. He has never once looked at the caller ID.

"Who is this?" he asks, though he knows the answer.

"It’s me," the voice says. There is no name. There is no explanation. Just a presence, steady as a heartbeat.

Arthur looks at the clock. 11:43 PM. He has been hosting this show for ten years. He has moved apartments, changed cars, buried a dog, and grown a beard he can no longer shave. But this voice has not aged. It has not cracked. It has not softened. It is a constant in a world of variables.

He should hang up. He should report it as a prank, a glitch, a haunting. But he doesn’t. He leans forward, the microphone picking up the creak of his chair. "What do you want?" he whispers.

"Nothing," the voice replies. "I just wanted to make sure you were still here."

The studio is cold. The city outside is a blur of neon and rain. Arthur realizes, with a sudden, icy shock, that he has never once asked the caller where they are. He has never once asked why they call. For a decade, he has treated this voice as a prop, a piece of furniture in the landscape of his broadcast. He has built his identity around the idea that he is the one who listens, the one who holds the space. But tonight, the dynamic shifts. The caller is not a guest. The caller is a mirror.

Arthur looks at the red light. It is still blinking. He realizes he is afraid. Not of the voice, but of the silence that will follow if he lets it go. He has spent ten years being the anchor, the fixed point. But if the caller is the only constant, then who is he? Just a man in a chair, talking to a ghost that refuses to die.

"Okay," Arthur says, his voice trembling. "I’m here."

The line goes dead. The static returns, empty and vast. Arthur sits in the dark, the red light blinking on, waiting for the next night, knowing he will be here, and knowing, with a certainty that feels like a blade in his gut, that the voice will be there too.
