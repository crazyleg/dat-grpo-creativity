The fog rolled in thick and gray, swallowing the harbor until the only thing visible was the rusted hull of the *Maren*, sitting listless in the water. The captain, a man with a face like a crumpled map, announced through the crackling intercom that the crossing would be delayed by "a few minutes." Then, he went silent. The minutes stretched into hours.

At first, the passengers stood by the portholes, watching the nothingness. Then, out of boredom or perhaps a sudden, collective desire to escape the monotony, the lies began.

Old Mrs. Gable, who had been knitting a scarf that looked more like a mop, turned to the young man next to her. "I was a concert pianist," she said, her voice trembling with a fabricated confidence. "I played for the Queen in ’62. She cried. I made her cry."

The young man, a student with ink-stained fingers, smiled politely. "I’m a surgeon," he lied, adjusting his glasses. "I operate on hearts. Last week, I saved a man who had been dead for six minutes. The nurses were screaming. I was calm."

Mrs. Gable nodded, accepting the fiction. "I see the precision in your hands," she murmured.

Across the deck, a woman in a red coat began a monologue about her life as a deep-sea diver. She described the crushing pressure, the bioluminescent jellyfish, and the time she found a sunken submarine with the crew still alive. She spoke with such vivid detail that a child nearby stopped crying and stared at her, mesmerized.

The lies multiplied like mold. A man claimed to be a former astronaut who had seen the edge of the universe. A teenager confessed to being a fugitive from a country that no longer existed. A couple whispered about a secret marriage that had never happened, their eyes locking in a shared, invented intimacy.

No one corrected anyone. The fog outside was a blank canvas, and the passengers were painting their own portraits on it. The truth, with its dull edges and unglamorous details, felt too heavy to carry in the damp air. Instead, they built a city of falsehoods, a fragile structure of words that held together only because everyone agreed to believe it.

When the fog finally lifted, the *Maren* lurched forward, the engine groaning to life. The passengers straightened, their postures shifting. The pianist returned to her knitting. The surgeon checked his watch. The diver adjusted her coat.

The lies didn’t vanish; they simply folded themselves into the pockets of their minds, waiting for the next delay, the next fog, the next moment when the truth was too cold to bear. As the ferry crossed the open water, the passengers sat in silence, each carrying a secret that was no longer their own, bound together by the shared, unspoken agreement that, for now, the story was enough.
