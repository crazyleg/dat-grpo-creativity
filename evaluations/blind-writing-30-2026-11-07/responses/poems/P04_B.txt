The kettle hums its silver note,
a thread of steam that curls and dies.
I stir the sugar, count the grains,
and watch the morning light
slide slow across the empty chair.
Your shadow, long ago,
still lingers in the dust
that settles on the windowsill.
I fold the letters, never read,
and lock them in the drawer
where winter keeps its silence.
The clock ticks, soft and sure,
a heartbeat I no longer feel.
Outside, the rain begins to fall,
and I pour two cups of tea,
then drink the second one alone.
