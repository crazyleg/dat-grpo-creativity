The window frame holds the afternoon light,
A geometry of gold and dust.
Your coffee cup sits on the saucer,
Still warm, though you have left the room.

I trace the rim where your lips were,
A ghost of pressure on the porcelain.
The clock ticks its indifferent rhythm,
Counting seconds I do not wish to spend.

Outside, the rain begins to fall,
Blurring the world into gray water.
I watch the droplets race the glass,
Merging, separating, merging again.

There is no word for this specific weight,
This hollow space beside the pillow.
Just the silence, thick and heavy,
Hanging in the air like smoke.
