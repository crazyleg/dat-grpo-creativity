The house exhales a long, slow breath,
a creaking spine of oak and pine,
settling deeper into earth.
The pipes, once loud with living voices,
now hum a low, metallic lullaby,
cooling in the dark.
A windowpane, a single pane,
taps softly at the glass,
a ghost of wind that forgot its name.
The clock, that faithful, ticking sentinel,
marks the hours in a hollow chime,
measuring the distance
between the sleeping and the waking.
In this suspended, silent architecture,
the walls hold their breath,
waiting for the sun to wake them.
