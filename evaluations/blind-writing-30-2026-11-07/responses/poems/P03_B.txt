I hold the dust of feet that never roam,
The echo of a thousand quiet homes.
My shadows stretch across the cracked asphalt,
Where lives are small, yet deeply, fiercely kept.
They know the cracks by name and number,
The specific way the morning light does blur.
No train, no ticket, no distant shore,
Just the rhythm of the open door.
They trade in glances, not in gold,
A story told, then softly folded.
My streets are narrow, yet they feel like seas,
Where roots run deep beneath the knees.
I am the map they never need to read,
The home they build, and the place they bleed.
In every window, a life remains,
Anchored tight within my urban veins.
