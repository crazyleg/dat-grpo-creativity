I stood on the pool’s edge, a trembling leaf,
The water cold, a wall of liquid glass.
I’d spent a lifetime learning how to breathe,
But not how to let go, to let it pass.
The instructor’s voice, a steady, calm refrain,
Said, "Trust the current. Do not fight the deep."
I closed my eyes and surrendered to the rain
Of splashing drops that kissed my face and sleep.
My limbs, so stiff with fear, began to float,
A strange and buoyant, weightless, strange release.
I learned that sinking is not always a vote
For drowning, but a pause, a gentle peace.
The chlorine stung, but I could feel the tide
Lift me, a ghost, on the other side.
