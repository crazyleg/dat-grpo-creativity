The clock ticks soft against the wall,
a metronome for a held breath.
I trace the rim of my cold cup,
where the steam has long since died.
No name is spoken, only numbers
floating in the sterile air,
a verdict wrapped in sterile white.
The window shows the rain, a blur,
as if the world is waiting too,
its colors muted, paused in gray.
I count the seconds, one by one,
a private, silent, endless drum.
The silence stretches, thin and tight,
a thread of hope, a thread of fear.
I do not ask what it may be,
only if I’ll wake to light.
