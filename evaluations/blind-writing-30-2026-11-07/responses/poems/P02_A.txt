O "surrender," soft and old,
Why do you rust in the vault of the mind?
Once, you were the key to the lock,
A gentle yielding, a peace left behind.
Now, you are a ghost in the grammar,
A syllable caught in the throat of the crowd,
No longer the bridge between the storm
And the calm, quiet, surrendering shroud.
I hear you in the sigh of the leaf
That lets go of the branch without a fight,
In the hand that releases the rope,
In the heart that forgives the slight.
Come back, you word of the ancient tongue,
We have forgotten how to let go,
How to lay down the heavy stone
And rest in the peace of the unknown.
