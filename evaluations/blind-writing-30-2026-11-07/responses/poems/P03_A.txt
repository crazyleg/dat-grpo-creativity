I am the cobblestone, worn smooth by the same
shuffling soles, the same slow, circular pace.
My gutters know the rhythm of their rain,
my lampposts hold the rust of their embrace.
They count the cracks in my facade like beads,
a rosary of dust and broken glass.
No train, no highway, no horizon bleeds
beyond the shadow of this single pass.
They speak in whispers that the walls absorb,
a language built from silence and decay.
I am the only world they have to rob,
the only sky that can betray.
My roots drink deep of their forgotten names,
my stones remember every single claim.
They are the city, and I am the frame,
two ghosts who never leave the same.
