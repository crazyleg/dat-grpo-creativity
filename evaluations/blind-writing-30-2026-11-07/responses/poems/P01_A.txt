The brass key turns, a final, heavy sigh,
I lock the door beneath the rising tide.
No more to climb the spiral, worn and steep,
No more to polish lenses, vast and deep.
The wick is trimmed, the oil is left to drain,
The clockwork heart begins to beat in vain.
Tomorrow, sensors wake to guard the shore,
No human hand to open, close the door.
I watch the beam, a slow and golden sweep,
A ghost of all the vigils I have kept.
The foghorn sleeps; the sea will not be kind,
But I have left the dark behind.
I take my coat, my boots, my final breath,
And walk into the morning, out of death.
