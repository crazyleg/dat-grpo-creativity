The pool’s edge holds its breath, a cold blue wall,
Where childhood fears are buried, deep and tall.
I grip the lane line, knuckles white and tight,
Expecting panic, drowning in the light.
But lungs remember what the mind has lost,
A rhythmic surge, a heavy, floating cost.
The water parts, then closes over me,
A strange embrace of cold and liberty.
No more the frantic flailing of the young,
Just steady strokes where silence belongs.
I rise, gasping, alive and wet and free,
Reclaiming breath from the deep and blue.
